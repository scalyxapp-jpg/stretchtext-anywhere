A text that answers back

In 1967 Ted Nelson described a kind of writing that does not unfold all at once: a passage that opens where the reader wants more, and closes where they already know. He called it stretchtext, and for fifty years almost nobody used it, because every level had to be written by hand.

The reason is arithmetic. Three levels of detail turn one paragraph into eighteen, and the writer usually knows only the first. What made stretchtext impractical was never the reading. It was the writing.

Generated text changes that arithmetic. A reader can ask for the next level at the exact place the question appears, and get it there, without leaving the sentence they were already reading.

Reading about a sixth of the words, and understanding more of them. That is the promise of a text that only grows where you pull.

Try it. Select a phrase below, or anywhere on this page, and press Stretch. The passage is replaced by a longer one; the underline marks what grew. Click it to put the original back.

A river slows where it widens, drops what it carries, and leaves a new bank behind.

Install

  1. Clone the repository.
  2. Open chrome://extensions and turn on Developer mode.
  3. Choose Load unpacked and pick the extension/ folder inside the repository — that is where manifest.json lives.
  4. Click the toolbar icon, then Connect with Pollinations.

Reads are paid with the reader's own Pollen: the extension holds a scoped key for seven days and a five-pollen budget. No server, no account of ours, no telemetry.
